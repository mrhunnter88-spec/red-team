🧠 The Mathematical Puzzle: Constellation EVM Shifting

In standard high-speed wireless protocols (like Wi-Fi), data is mapped onto a geometric grid called a Constellation Diagram using Quadrature Amplitude Modulation (QAM). For instance, in Quadrature Phase Shift Keying (QPSK), every symbol represents two bits mapped to ideal complex coordinates (I = In-Phase, Q = Quadrature).
An RF steganographic transmitter takes the ideal target point and shifts it slightly depending on the hidden bit it wants to smuggle:
• To hide a 0: Shift the symbol slightly closer to the graph's origin (lower amplitude).
• To hide a 1: Shift the symbol slightly further from the graph's origin (higher amplitude).
Because the shift is incredibly small, a standard commercial Wi-Fi card processes the symbol normally. It rounds the coordinate to the nearest ideal point and drops the tiny variation, viewing it as standard environmental thermal noise. A forensic SDR receiver, however, measures this exact distance variation—known as the **Error Vector—**and extracts the hidden binary array.

🛡️ Forensic Defense & EVM Monitoring

To detect covert channels hidden inside standard enterprise or military wireless networks, defense systems implement physical-layer anomaly tracking:
1. Baseline EVM Profiling: Wireless Intrusion Detection Systems (WIDS) track the Error Vector Magnitude (EVM) across the network. If an environment is quiet, the EVM scatter plot around a constellation point should be perfectly random and uniform. If an unauthorized steganographic channel is operating, the EVM metrics will show structured, bimodal clustering (grouping explicitly inward or outward), instantly alerting analysts to a data exfiltration attempt.
2. Strict Channel State Information (CSI) Auditing: Advanced firewalls profile the fine-grained physical layer properties of multipath radio reflections (CSI). If the CSI phase responses of successive frames show high entropy or periodic adjustments that do not correspond to the physical movements of objects in the room, the layer-1 controller flags a covert channel anomaly.
